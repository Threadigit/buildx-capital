# Policy Tracker methodology

The Policy Tracker translates government action into distributional and operating consequences.

## Policy selection

We track policies that materially change household economics, business costs, market access, investment conditions or the provision of essential services. The tracker begins with Nigeria and expands across Africa as coverage deepens.

## Sources

The starting point is always the legal or administrative instrument: legislation, regulation, executive order, budget document, tariff decision or official implementation guidance. We distinguish between an announcement, a rule that has taken legal effect and a rule that is actually being enforced.

## Distributional test

Each policy is assessed across three broad groups:

- **Upper class:** asset owners, high earners and firms with access to capital or alternatives
- **Middle class:** salaried households, formal-sector professionals and small business owners
- **Base of the pyramid:** low-income and informal households with limited buffers

These groups are analytical lenses, not claims that every person within them experiences policy in the same way.

## Response

For each group, we identify the direct effect, second-order effects, timing, available options and risks of inaction. Recommendations are bounded by the evidence and distinguish defensive moves from genuine opportunity.

Every note separates the policy read from the application section so the analysis remains useful before and after paid access begins.
